A Design Science of the Artificial

Bridging Academic Theory & Contemporary Practice

Traditional social sciences excel at describing "what is." Educational Design Research (EDR) is an interventionist paradigm engineered to design and study "what ought to be."

Epistemological Shift

Escape the "Sisyphus Trap" of Classical Research

For over half a century, traditional educational research was constrained by a severe credibility gap. By isolating variables in laboratory settings to establish artificial controls, educational inquiry produced context-free findings that routinely crumbled when introduced into messy, complex, real-world classrooms.

DBR rejects this decontextualized paradigm. Rooted in Herbert Simon's (1969) Foundations of Design Science, it treats educational environments not as natural systems to be passively described, but as artificial systems engineered to accomplish human and value-driven pedagogical goals.

"The natural sciences are concerned with how things are... Design, on the other hand, is concerned with how things ought to be."
— Herbert Simon, Sciences of the Artificial

The Paradigmatic Divide

01

Natural & Social Science

Descriptive, value-neutral inquiry. Concentrates strictly on studying "what is" via isolated variables.

02

Humanistic & Philosophical

Critical interpretive scholarship. Rich in conceptual values but lacks rigorous empirical validation.

03

Design Science (DBR)

Active, use-inspired intervention. Simultaneously designs robust practical tools and advances learning theory.

Myth vs. Reality

Dismantling DBR Misconceptions

Because DBR operates at the intersection of design engineering and learning sciences, it is frequently subjected to misunderstandings. Select a misconception below to see its rigorous scientific rebuttal.

Critique / Misconception

"It's not really scientific, because the researcher is not neutral and you don't have a control group."

"How can subjective, participant-observer research yield reliable findings when standard control models are completely omitted?"

Rigorous Rebuttal

Standard clinical experiments try to "randomize away" context, but context, cognition, and tools are irreducibly co-constituted in learning. DBR maintains scientific rigor through systematic triangulation across multiple data sources and retrospective analysis, creating an audit trail that guarantees reliability through virtual replicability (trackability).

The Road Ahead

Addressing Systemic Chokepoints

While DBR has grown in academic recognition, the design-science community is actively developing frameworks to tackle its remaining bottlenecks.

01

The Scaling Chasm

Interventions that perform flawlessly in the protective, highly-supported "Alpha" phase often collapse during widespread, un-scaffolded adoption.

Direction: DBIR

Transitioning to Design-Based Implementation Research (DBIR), co-negotiating policy alignment and school systems structures to design for "graceful degradation".

02

The Reporting Deficit

Literature reviews reveal up to 40% of published DBR studies fail to report cycle counts or detail how designs evolved between iterations.

Direction: Transparency

Advocating for formalized design logging templates in top-tier journals (such as EDeR) and sharing raw, retrospective datasets.

03

Academic Mismatch

Tenure systems favor abstract journal paper counts over robust, usable educational designs, rendering EDR highly taxing for doctoral students.

Direction: Engineering Rights

Partnering with societies like the ISDDE to lobby committees to assess design-researchers on practicing impact alongside theoretical outputs.

Educational Design Research

Reconnecting classroom learning theory and actionable design patterns to drive sustainable pedagogical change.
